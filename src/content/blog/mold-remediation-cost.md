## How much does mold remediation cost in Raleigh, NC?

Mold remediation in Raleigh typically costs $500 to $6,000, with most single-room jobs falling between $1,200 and $3,000. The final number depends on square footage, whether materials like drywall or subfloor decking need to be removed, and whether the job requires full poly containment and negative-air HEPA filtration.

On a per-square-foot basis, remediation work generally runs $10 to $25 per square foot of affected area, which is why a 10-square-foot bathroom patch and a 1,200-square-foot crawl space land in very different price brackets even though both are "mold remediation." Jobs priced per square foot also usually include setup and teardown of containment, not just the cleaning itself.

## What factors change the price of mold remediation?

The biggest cost drivers are square footage, what the mold is growing on, and how hard the space is to access. A technician crawling through 18 inches of clearance under a Historic Oakwood bungalow takes longer and costs more than the same square footage in an open basement.

Porous materials like drywall, subfloor, and insulation usually get removed and replaced rather than cleaned, because mold grows into the fibers. Non-porous surfaces like framing lumber, concrete, and HVAC ductwork can often be cleaned and treated in place, which costs less. Jobs that need full containment barriers, negative air machines, and post-remediation clearance testing also run higher than a quick spot treatment.

Homes in Raleigh's older neighborhoods, including the bungalows around Historic Oakwood and streets near NC State University, often pair crawl space mold remediation with a vapor barrier or full encapsulation to keep the problem from coming back. ## Will homeowners insurance pay for mold remediation?

Most standard homeowners policies cover mold remediation only when the mold resulted from a sudden, covered water loss, such as a burst pipe, not from long-term humidity, a slow leak, or poor crawl space ventilation. Even when it's covered, many policies cap mold-related payouts at $1,000 to $10,000.

That gap matters in Raleigh, where humid summers and older crawl spaces mean a lot of mold growth is gradual rather than sudden. The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) outlines how standard policies draw this line between sudden, accidental damage and long-term maintenance issues. If your mold followed a specific, documentable event like an appliance failure, photograph the source, keep the plumber's or restoration company's report, and file promptly. If it developed slowly, expect to pay out of pocket and treat the remediation cost as the real number to plan around.

## What happens during a mold remediation job?

A professional mold remediation job follows a defined sequence set by the [ANSI/IICRC S520 standard](https://iicrc.org/s520/), the industry standard for mold remediation. Skipping steps is how mold comes back.

1. **Inspection and moisture source identification.** A tech confirms the extent of growth and finds what's keeping the area wet, because remediation without fixing the moisture source is temporary.
2. **Containment setup.** Poly sheeting isolates the work area so spores don't spread to the rest of the home during removal.
3. **Negative air and HEPA filtration.** Air scrubbers run continuously to capture airborne spores while work is underway.
4. **Removal of affected porous materials.** Moldy drywall, insulation, and carpet padding get bagged and removed, not scraped and reused.
5. **Cleaning and antimicrobial treatment.** Remaining non-porous surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated.
6. **Drying.** The area is brought back to a normal moisture reading before anything gets closed back up.
7. **Clearance testing.** A post-remediation air or surface sample confirms spore counts are back to a normal range before the job is called done.
