## Restoration emergencies common in Elm City

Elm City sits on the flat, sandy Coastal Plain of eastern North Carolina, and that terrain is part of why water damage shows up here differently than it does further inland. Heavy rain from tropical systems and summer thunderstorms doesn't drain quickly across this flat ground, so yards, crawlspaces, and low-lying lots hold water longer after a storm than homeowners expect. Combine that with a mix of older frame homes on pier-and-crawlspace foundations and a humid subtropical climate, and you get conditions where a slow roof leak or a crawlspace intrusion can turn into a mold problem within a couple of days rather than weeks. Hurricane season, roughly June through November, brings the sharpest spike in storm damage and wind-driven water intrusion calls for the area.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Elm City

Elm City's housing stock is a mix of early-to-mid-20th-century frame construction near the older part of town and newer single-story and manufactured homes on the surrounding rural lots. Crawlspace foundations dominate the older stock, with slab-on-grade more common in newer builds. Plumbing varies accordingly: expect galvanized or cast iron supply and drain lines in homes from before the 1970s, with copper and PEX in anything renovated or built more recently.
